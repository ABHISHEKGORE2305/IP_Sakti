Section 1: Formulation Classification & Drug Regulatory Pathways
Q101
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Classical Ayurvedic Medicine

Question: A startup wants to manufacture and sell Chyawanprash strictly following the formula and method described in the Sahasrayogam (a First-Schedule text). What is its regulatory classification, and can they patent the formulation?

Benchmark Answer:

Classification: Classical / Generic Ayurvedic Medicine under Section 3(a) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, as it is manufactured strictly according to an authoritative text listed in the First Schedule.

IP Posture: Cannot be patented. It is barred under Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, 1970 (an invention which in effect is traditional knowledge or an aggregation/duplication of known properties of traditionally known component/s).

Protection Mechanism: Defended via the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) to prevent misappropriation.

System Action: Flag Section 3(p) patent bar; direct user to obtaining a manufacturing license under Form 25D of Drugs & Cosmetics Rules.

Q102
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Patent & Proprietary Medicine (P&P)

Question: An applicant adds modern excipients and two additional herbal extracts (Curcuma longa and Piper nigrum) to a classical Triphala ratio. How is this classified, and what is its patent status?

Benchmark Answer:

Classification: Patent or Proprietary Medicine under Section 3(h) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 (contains ingredients mentioned in First Schedule texts but prepared in non-traditional ratios/combinations).

IP Posture: Subject to Section 3(e) (mere admixture) and Section 3(p) objections. To be patentable, the applicant must demonstrate non-obvious synergistic efficacy over and above the additive effects of the individual components.

System Action: Trigger Formulation Switch to P&P; state requirement for comparative trial data showing synergy to overcome Section 3(e)/3(p) objections.

Q103
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Phytopharmaceutical Drug

Question: What is the distinction between a Phytopharmaceutical Drug under the Drugs & Cosmetics Rules and a Patent/Proprietary Ayurvedic Medicine?

Benchmark Answer:

Phytopharmaceutical Drug: Defined under Rule 2(eb) of Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945. Requires a purified, standardized extract of a medicinal plant (or part) containing at least 4 active/marker compounds, evaluated via modern clinical trials (Phase I, II, III) under New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019.

P&P Ayurvedic Medicine: Formulated using whole plants/extracts based on Ayurvedic principles without requiring chemical isolation of 4 marker compounds; governed under Chapter IV-A of Drugs & Cosmetics Act.

IP Difference: Phytopharmaceuticals have high patentability potential (novel extraction, purification, fraction composition) under Section 3(d)/3(e).

Q104
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: New / Non-Classical Drug

Question: A researcher isolates a novel polysaccharide fraction from Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia) for targeted immunotherapy. Is this an Ayurvedic drug or a New Drug?

Benchmark Answer:

Classification: New Drug / Phytopharmaceutical / Modern Chemical Entity depending on processing. If isolated as a purified chemical fraction requiring safety/efficacy profiling via modern toxicological and clinical studies, it falls under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019.

IP Posture: High patent potential. Section 3(p) does not apply if the isolated bioactive fraction exhibits a technical effect/efficacy not known in traditional literature.

Q105
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Food / Ayurveda Aahar

Question: Can an Ayush company market an Ashwagandha herbal tea mix as an "Ayurveda Aahar"? What are the key statutory boundaries?

Benchmark Answer:

Classification: Ayurveda Aahar under Food Safety and Standards (Ayurveda Aahar) Regulations, 2022.

Statutory Boundaries:

Must contain ingredients specified in authoritative Ayurvedic texts listed in Schedule A of the FSSAI regulation.

Cannot make disease treatment/cure claims (violates Drugs & Magic Remedies Act).

Excludes synthetic vitamins, minerals, or isolated amino acids.

IP Posture: Trademarks and brand packaging designs apply; formulation itself is non-patentable TK.

Q106
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Cosmetics

Question: A firm manufactures a face cream incorporating Kumkumadi Tailam. Does this fall under Ayurvedic drug regulations or cosmetic regulations?

Benchmark Answer:

Classification: Cosmetic under Section 3(aaa) of Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, governed by the Cosmetics Rules, 2020 and IS 15000 standards.

Condition: If therapeutic claims (e.g., treats psoriasis, eczema) are made, it defaults back to an Ayurvedic Drug (Chapter IV-A). If restricted to beautification/cleansing, it remains a Cosmetic.

IP Posture: Industrial design rights for packaging; trademark protection; extract processing patent if novel.

Q107
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Novel Drug Delivery Systems (NDDS)

Question: An Ayurvedic laboratory encapsulates Curcumin into lipid nanoparticles for oral delivery. How does this impact classification and patentability?

Benchmark Answer:

Classification: Patent or Proprietary Medicine / NDDS Phytopharmaceutical.

IP Posture: Highly Patentable. While Curcumin and its medicinal properties are prior art / TK, the nano-formulation, carrier matrix, targeted release mechanism, and bio-availability improvement overcome Section 3(p) and Section 3(d) objections if unexpected technical advantages are proven.

Q108
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Adulteration / Regulatory Violation

Question: An Ayurvedic manufacturer adds 0.5 mg of synthetic Sildenafil to a classical Vajikarana formulation to enhance quick efficacy. What is the legal status?

Benchmark Answer:

Legal Status: Illegal. Classified as Adulterated Drug under Section 17A and Spurious Drug under Section 17B of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.

Consequences: Criminal prosecution under Section 27, cancellation of manufacturing license, immediate product recall, and total invalidation of any patent or trademark applications filed for the formulation.

Q109
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Extraction Technology

Question: A firm uses Supercritical Fluid Extraction (SFE) using CO₂ to extract active lipids from Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri). Is the process patentable?

Benchmark Answer:

Process Patentability: Yes, eligible for a process patent under Section 2(1)(j) if the SFE process parameters (temperature, pressure, co-solvent ratio) produce a unique, reproducible profile not previously disclosed.

Product Patentability: The extract itself must overcome Section 3(d) (enhanced efficacy) and Section 3(p) (TK bar).

Q110
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Polyherbal Formulations

Question: An MSME combines equal proportions of Tulsi, Ginger, and Adhatoda vasica for cough relief. Is this patentable if no single herb is claimed individually?

Benchmark Answer:

IP Posture: Direct invocation of Section 3(e) (mere admixture) and Section 3(p).

Requirement: The applicant must provide comparative experimental data demonstrating that the combination exhibits a synergistic therapeutic index significantly greater than the mathematical sum of the individual herbs. Without synergy data, the IPO will issue an irrebuttable rejection.

Q111
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Classical Medicine Variant

Question: A company replaces sucrose syrup with a stevia-based sugar-free base in classical Vasavaleha. Does it retain classical status?

Benchmark Answer:

Classification: Shifts from Classical Medicine to Patent or Proprietary (P&P) Medicine.

Reason: Altering excipients or vehicle preparation methods from the authoritative First Schedule text removes it from pure classical status under Section 3(a).

IP Status: Base composition/sugar-free vehicle combination can be protected via industrial trade secret or formulation patent if synergy/stability is non-obvious.

Q112
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Bio-enhancers

Question: An applicant claims a patent for adding Piperine (from Black Pepper) to Curcumin to increase bioavailability by 2000%. Is this patentable in India?

Benchmark Answer:

IP Status: Highly scrutinized under Section 3(d) and 3(p).

Precedent: Bio-availability enhancement using Piperine is well-documented in existing scientific prior art and traditional Ayurvedic concepts (Anupana).

Requirement: Patentability requires proving a novel, non-obvious chemical modification or specific narrow ratio showing unexpected bio-enhancement beyond known prior art disclosures.

Q113
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Route of Administration

Question: Can an Ayurvedic manufacturer create an injectable formulation of an Ayurvedic extract?

Benchmark Answer:

Regulatory Bar: Classical Ayurvedic texts do not recognize parenteral/injectable administration (Injectables are explicitly excluded under traditional Ayush definitions).

Classification Shift: Such a product immediately falls under Allopathic New Drug / Phytopharmaceutical Regulations requiring full Phase I-III clinical trials under the Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO).

Q114
Jurisdiction Focus: System / User Interaction

Domain: Clarification Protocol

Question: What minimum questions must the chatbot ask to classify an unlabelled Ayush product submitted by a user?

Benchmark Answer:

Source Origin: Is the formula taken verbatim from a First Schedule text (e.g., Charaka Samhita, Sharangadhara)?

Ingredients: Does it contain synthetic chemical compounds or only plant/animal/mineral extracts?

Intended Claim: Is it marketed for disease cure/treatment, general well-being/food, or cosmetic application?

Route/Form: Is it an oral/topical traditional dose form or a novel nano/injectable system?

Q115
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Micro-encapsulation

Question: A firm micro-encapsulates Kshirabala Tailam in gelatin capsules for targeted enteropathy delivery. What is the IP and regulatory posture?

Benchmark Answer:

Regulatory: Patent or Proprietary Medicine / Novel Drug Delivery System.

IP Posture: The oil itself is traditional knowledge (Section 3(p) bar), but the micro-encapsulation process and targeted release capsule matrix can be patented as a novelty under Section 2(1)(j).

Section 2: Indian Patents Act & TKDL / Section 3(p)
Q116
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Patents Act Section 3(p)

Question: Define Section 3(p) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970 and its significance for Ayurvedic inventions.

Benchmark Answer:

Definition: Section 3(p) excludes from patentability "an invention which in effect is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components."

Significance: Protects India's bio-cultural heritage and prevents biopiracy by ensuring public domain Ayurvedic remedies are not monopolized by private entities.

Q117
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Patent Objections

Question: How does the Patent Office distinguish between a Section 3(e) rejection and a Section 3(p) rejection for a polyherbal product?

Benchmark Answer:

Section 3(e): Objects on grounds that the product is a mere admixture resulting only in aggregation of properties of components (focuses on lack of synergistic efficacy).

Section 3(p): Objects on grounds that the components or their therapeutic uses are already part of Traditional Knowledge in public domain literature (focuses on lack of novelty over TK).

Q118
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Patent Defense Strategy

Question: How can an applicant successfully overcome a Section 3(p) objection raised in a First Examination Report (FER)?

Benchmark Answer:

Demonstrate that the specific combination, processing step, or bio-fraction is not disclosed in any authoritative TK text or TKDL database entry.

Provide quantitative experimental evidence showing unexpected synergistic synergy or a new, non-obvious technical effect (e.g., specific molecular target action).

Narrow claims from the raw herb/extract formulation to specific modified, novel bio-active fractions or novel delivery structures.

Q119
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: TKDL / Prior Art

Question: What is the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL), and how is it used during patent examination at the Indian Patent Office (IPO)?

Benchmark Answer:

TKDL: A digital repository created by CSIR and Ministry of Ayush translating ancient Sanskrit, Unani, Siddha, and Arabic texts into 5 international languages (English, Japanese, French, German, Spanish).

Role at IPO: Examiners search TKDL during novelty and inventive step assessments. If an invention matches a TKDL record, it is cited as prior art under Section 3(p).

Q120
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Novel Extraction Patents

Question: Can a process for extracting Withanolide D from Withania somnifera using a specific temperature-pressure solvent gradient be patented in India?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. While Withania somnifera (Ashwagandha) and its general uses are TK (Section 3(p)), a specific, non-obvious process that optimizes yield, purity, or stability of a bio-active molecule through unique parameters is a patentable process under Section 2(1)(j), provided it satisfies novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability.

Q121
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Isolated Bioactive Molecules

Question: Is a pure chemical compound isolated from a traditional medicinal plant patentable per se under Indian Patent Law?

Benchmark Answer:

Scrutiny under Section 3(d) & 3(c): Section 3(c) bars discovery of naturally occurring substances. Isolating a naturally occurring compound without structural alteration is considered a discovery.

Requirement: To be patentable, the isolated compound must show structural modifications or new unexpected efficacy over the crude form, overcoming Section 3(d) and Section 3(c).

Q122
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Patent Rules 2024 / Expedited Examination

Question: Can an AYUSH startup avail expedited examination of its patent application under the Patents (Amendment) Rules, 2024?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. Recognized startups (DPIIT) and small entities/MSMEs can file Form 18A for Expedited Examination.

Requirement: Must meet criteria such as choosing India as the International Searching Authority (ISA) or qualifying as a DPIIT-recognized startup, dramatically reducing examination timelines from years to months.

Q123
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Compulsory Licensing

Question: Under what conditions can a compulsory license be issued for a patented Ayurvedic-derived drug under Section 84 of the Patents Act?

Benchmark Answer:

Conditions (after 3 years from grant):

Reasonable requirements of the public with respect to the patented invention have not been satisfied.

Patented invention is not available to the public at a reasonably affordable price.

Patented invention is not worked in the territory of India.

Q124
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Form 1 Disclosure

Question: What specific declaration regarding biological resources and TK must be made in Form 1 of an Indian patent application?

Benchmark Answer:

Applicants must explicitly declare in Form 1/specification whether the invention uses biological material from India.

If yes, the source and geographical origin must be disclosed, and a declaration confirming approval from the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) under Section 6 of the BD Act must be submitted prior to patent grant.

Q125
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Section 3(h) Agricultural Exclusions

Question: An Ayush firm invents a specialized organic method for growing high-yield Tulsi plants. Can they patent this agricultural method?

Benchmark Answer:

No. Barred under Section 3(h) of the Patents Act, 1970, which excludes "a method of agriculture or horticulture."

Alternative Protection: Registration of plant varieties under the Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act (PPVFRA), 2001.

Q126
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Patent Evidence Requirements

Question: What statistical/experimental evidence does the IPO require to validate claims of "synergistic effect" in polyherbal formulations?

Benchmark Answer:

The IPO requires comparative in-vitro / in-vivo clinical trial data demonstrating that Combination (A+B) achieves a statistically significant therapeutic response greater than the arithmetic sum of individual components A and B evaluated at identical dosages (E(AB)>E(A)+E(B)).

Q127
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Patent Rules 2024 Changes

Question: How do the Patents (Amendment) Rules, 2024 affect statement and undertaking obligations under Section 8 (Form 3)?

Benchmark Answer:

Under the 2024 Rules, the deadline and frequency for updating Form 3 (details of corresponding foreign filings) have been relaxed. Applicants now file Form 3 within 3 months of the first examination report (FER) issuance rather than continuous periodic updates, reducing administrative compliance burdens.

Q128
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Pre-Grant Opposition

Question: Who can file a Pre-Grant Opposition under Section 25(1) against an Ayurvedic patent application, and on what TKDL grounds?

Benchmark Answer:

Any person can file a pre-grant opposition under Section 25(1) after publication but before grant.

Grounds: Section 25(1)(k) — that the complete specification does not disclose or wrongly mentions the source or geographical origin of biological material; or Section 25(1)(e/f) using TKDL records showing anticipation by public knowledge/TK.

Q129
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Post-Grant Opposition

Question: Can a granted patent for an Ayurvedic drug extract be revoked under Section 64 for non-disclosure of biological source?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. Under Section 64(1)(p) of the Patents Act, 1970, a patent can be revoked by the High Court/Controller if the complete specification failed to disclose or wrongly stated the geographical origin/source of biological material used for the invention.

Q130
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Diagnostic Devices

Question: A hardware device uses sensor arrays to automate Nadi Pariksha (pulse diagnosis). Is this patentable under Section 3(k) or Section 3(i)?

Benchmark Answer:

Hardware Device: Patentable if novel and inventive (sensor mechanisms, physical diagnostic apparatus).

Software/Algorithm: Subject to Section 3(k) (computer program per se) and Section 3(i) (diagnostic method). Must demonstrate a technical contribution or hardware integration (e.g., specific bio-sensor system) beyond pure algorithm software.

Section 3: Biological Diversity Act (BDA 2023 Amendment) & ABS
Q131
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: BDA 2023 Exemption

Question: Are registered Ayurvedic practitioners (Vaidyas) required to pay Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) under the Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023?

Benchmark Answer:

No. The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 explicitly exempts registered AYUSH practitioners, codifiers, and users of traditional knowledge from prior approval and ABS obligations for practicing or manufacturing Ayush formulations.

Q132
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: BDA Section 6 Approval

Question: At what stage must an Indian company obtain National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) approval when applying for a patent involving biological resources?

Benchmark Answer:

Under Section 6(1) as amended in 2023, approval from the National Biodiversity Authority must be obtained before the grant of the patent, rather than at the time of filing the patent application.

Q133
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Domestic vs Foreign Entity ABS

Question: How does the Biological Diversity Act distinguish between a domestic Indian AYUSH MSME and a foreign-owned corporation regarding bio-resource access?

Benchmark Answer:

Domestic Indian Companies/Entities: Under BDA 2023, domestic entities require notification/intimation to State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) for commercial utilization, but enjoy streamlined access and reduced ABS rates.

Foreign Entities (Section 3(2)): Non-citizens, foreign corporations, or Indian companies with foreign equity participation must obtain prior NBA approval before accessing biological resources or filing IP.

Q134
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Research Results Transfer

Question: Does transferring research results on an Ayurvedic herb to a foreign university require NBA approval under Section 4 of BDA?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. Under Section 4 of the BDA, no person shall transfer research results relating to biological resources occurring in India to a foreign national/entity without prior approval of the NBA, except for collaborative research projects approved by the Central Government under Section 5.

Q135
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Cultivated Medicinal Plants

Question: Are cultivated medicinal plants (e.g., farmed Amla) subject to ABS compliance under the BDA 2023 Amendment?

Benchmark Answer:

Exempted. Cultivated medicinal plants and their products are exempted from ABS regulations under the 2023 Amendment, provided the producer/buyer obtains a Certificate of Origin or registration from the designated authorities.

Q136
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: BD Rules 2024 Forms

Question: Which form must be submitted to the NBA for obtaining approval prior to patent grant under Biological Diversity Rules, 2024?

Benchmark Answer:

Form III (Application for seeking approval of NBA for applying for/obtaining IPR based on biological resource/knowledge) under the Biological Diversity Rules.

Q137
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Legal Penalties

Question: What is the legal penalty for violating ABS requirements under the Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023?

Benchmark Answer:

Decriminalized: The 2023 Amendment decriminalized offenses (removed imprisonment). Violations now carry civil penalties ranging from ₹1 Lakh to ₹50 Lakhs (and up to ₹1 Crore for continuing violations), adjudicated by an Adjudicating Officer appointed by the Central Government.

Q138
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: BMC Local Sharing

Question: What role do Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) play in local benefit sharing?

Benchmark Answer:

BMCs are constituted by local bodies to prepare People’s Biodiversity Registers (PBRs). A portion of the ABS funds collected by NBA/SBB is mandated to be passed down directly to local BMCs and benefit-claimers in the region where the bio-resource was harvested.

Q139
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Bio-resource Export

Question: Can an Indian company export raw Guduchi stems abroad for extraction research without NBA approval?

Benchmark Answer:

No. Exporting biological resources sourced from India to foreign countries for research or commercial utilization requires explicit prior approval from the NBA under Section 3 of the BDA.

Q140
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Mandi Sourcing Compliance

Question: If an AYUSH MSME buys dried herbs from a local wholesale mandi (trader), how do they ensure ABS compliance?

Benchmark Answer:

Under BDA 2023, the MSME must maintain purchase receipts, invoices, and traceability documents showing whether the herb is cultivated (exempt) or wild-sourced. Intimation to the State Biodiversity Board (SBB) is required if sourced from wild populations for commercial scale production.

Q141
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Traditional Knowledge Practitioners

Question: Does an indigenous tribal healer sharing ancestral plant knowledge with an Ayush startup lose rights over that knowledge?

Benchmark Answer:

No. The BDA protects traditional knowledge holders. The startup must execute a Prior Informed Consent (PIC) and Mutually Agreed Terms (MAT) agreement ensuring equitable profit/benefit sharing with the healer prior to commercial exploitation.

Q142
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Foreign Equity Impact

Question: If a foreign venture capital firm buys a 15% equity stake in an Indian Ayush startup, does the startup's BDA status change?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. The startup becomes a "foreign-controlled company" under Section 3(2) of the BDA, shifting its compliance status from simple SBB intimation to requiring prior NBA approval for bio-resource access and IP grant.

Q143
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Regularization / Retroactive ABS

Question: Can an applicant apply for retrospective approval from NBA for a patent already applied for without prior clearance?

Benchmark Answer:

Under BDA 2023 and compliance guidelines, limited window provisions exist allowing applicants to seek regularization before patent grant by paying designated fees/surcharges, but non-compliance prior to grant leads to patent rejection.

Q144
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: ABS Quantum Calculation

Question: How is the ABS fee percentage calculated for commercial utilization of biological resources?

Benchmark Answer:

Under current ABS guidelines, benefit sharing for commercial utilization ranges from 0.1% to 0.5% of the annual gross ex-factory sale price of the product (excluding taxes), scaled based on annual turnover.

Q145
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Cultivated vs Wild Species

Question: What document proves that a batch of Ashwagandha is cultivated rather than wild-harvested to claim ABS exemption?

Benchmark Answer:

A Certificate of Origin or Cultivation Certificate issued by the State Forest/Agriculture Department, District Agriculture Officer, or AYUSH e-Aushadhi / Ayush Grid traceability portal.

Section 4: Trademarks, GIs, Designs, & PPVFRA
Q146
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Descriptive Trademarks

Question: Can an Ayush company register the trademark "Pure Triphala Churna" under Class 5?

Benchmark Answer:

No. Barred under Section 9(1)(a) and 9(1)(b) of the Trade Marks Act, 1999, as "Pure Triphala Churna" is generic, descriptive, and designates the kind/quality of the goods.

Permitted: Distinctive brand names containing "Triphala" as part of a unique logo or arbitrary brand word (e.g., "KALYANA-TRIPHALA" with distinct trade dress).

Q147
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Trademark Classification

Question: Under which Trademark Class should an Ayush enterprise register products spanning classical pills, herbal drinks, and skin creams?

Benchmark Answer:

Class 5: Ayurvedic medicines, pharmaceutical preparations, dietary supplements for medical use.

Class 32: Non-alcoholic herbal beverages/drinks.

Class 3: Herbal cosmetics, soaps, skincare preparations.

Class 30: Herbal teas, spices, food additives / Ayurveda Aahar.

Q148
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Geographical Indications (GI)

Question: Give an example of an Ayurvedic resource protected under Geographical Indications in India and its legal effect.

Benchmark Answer:

Example: Navara Rice (Kerala - medicinal rice used in Panchakarma), Kampillaka, or Vazhakulam Pineapple.

Legal Effect: Protects producers in the defined geographical territory against unauthorized usage under the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999. Prevents non-origin producers from using the location name.

Q149
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: GI Registered User

Question: Can a single Ayush startup "own" a Geographical Indication (GI) for an Ayurvedic herb?

Benchmark Answer:

No. A GI is a collective right owned by an association of producers or organization representing interest. A startup can only register as an Authorized User under Section 17 of the GI Act to commercialize the product under the GI tag.

Q150
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: GI vs Patent

Question: If Navara Rice has a GI, can an inventor patent a specialized starch gel extracted from it?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. The GI protects the geographical origin name and traditional identity of the raw crop. A novel, non-obvious starch gel process/product extracted from it can be patented separately, provided it satisfies patentability criteria and complies with NBA ABS requirements.

Q151
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Industrial Designs

Question: Can an Ayush company register an Industrial Design for an ergonomically shaped Shirodhara pot or custom bottle?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. Under the Designs Act, 2000, novelty in shape, configuration, pattern, ornament, or composition of lines applied to any article (e.g., custom bottle, applicator device) can be registered, provided it is purely aesthetic and non-functional.

Q152
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Plant Varieties (PPVFRA)

Question: How can an Ayush breeder protect a new, high-yield medicinal plant variety of Tulsi (Ocimum sanctum)?

Benchmark Answer:

Register under the Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act (PPVFRA), 2001.

Criteria: Must prove Distinctiveness, Uniformity, and Stability (DUS criteria) over existing varieties.

Q153
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Trade Dress Protection

Question: Can a company protect the specific green-and-gold color packaging scheme of its Ayurvedic formulation?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. Protected as Trade Dress under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 (under definition of "mark" including combination of colors/packaging) through passing-off actions or trademark registration if secondary meaning/distinctiveness is established.

Q154
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Well-Known Trademarks

Question: What constitutes a "Well-Known Trademark" for an Ayush enterprise in India?

Benchmark Answer:

Under Section 2(1)(zg) and Section 11 of Trade Marks Act, a mark recognized by a substantial segment of the public (e.g., iconic historic brands like Dabur, Baidyanath) enjoys protection across all trademark classes, preventing any third-party registration regardless of product category.

Q155
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Copyright Protection

Question: Is a new English translation and commentary of an ancient Sanskrit Ayurvedic manuscript protected under Copyright law?

Benchmark Answer:

Yes. While original Sanskrit root texts are in public domain, a new, original translation, arrangement, or commentary constitutes an original literary work under Section 13 of the Copyright Act, 1957.

Q156
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: GI Infringement Remedies

Question: What civil remedies exist for selling non-authentic rice under the "Navara Rice" GI tag?

Benchmark Answer:

Under Section 66/67 of the GI Act 1999: Injunctions, damages or account of profits, and forfeiture/destruction of infringing goods and packaging material.

Q157
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Trademark Objections (Section 11)

Question: What is a Section 11 objection during trademark examination of an Ayush brand?

Benchmark Answer:

Objection based on relative grounds for refusal—that the proposed mark is identical or deceptively similar to an existing prior registered trademark for similar goods/services (Class 5/3/30), creating likelihood of confusion among consumers.

Q158
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Trade Secrets

Question: How can an Ayush firm legally protect a secret, non-patented, specialized fermentation technique for Arishta preparation?

Benchmark Answer:

Protected as a Trade Secret via contractual Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs), non-compete clauses, access control protocols, and common law breach of confidence actions, as India lacks a standalone Trade Secret statute.

Q159
Jurisdiction Focus: International

Domain: Madrid Protocol

Question: How can an Indian Ayush startup protect its trademark in 10 foreign export countries simultaneously?

Benchmark Answer:

File an international application under the Madrid System (Madrid Protocol) administered by WIPO through the Indian Patent Office (IP India) as the office of origin, designating target member countries.

Q160
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Farmers' Rights in PPVFRA

Question: Does a plant variety protection under PPVFRA prevent traditional farmers from saving and re-sowing seeds of protected medicinal herbs?

Benchmark Answer:

No. Section 39(1)(iv) of PPVFRA safeguards Farmers' Rights, permitting farmers to save, use, sow, re-sow, exchange, or sell farm produce (including seed of a protected variety), provided they do not sell branded seed.

Section 5: Allied Regimes - FSSAI, DMR Act, & Labelling
Q161
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: FSSAI Ayurveda Aahar

Question: What is the specific legal definition of "Ayurveda Aahar" under FSSAI 2022 Regulations?

Benchmark Answer:

Food prepared in accordance with the recipes/methods described in authoritative books of Ayurveda listed in Schedule A of FSSAI (Ayurveda Aahar) Regulations 2022, intended for human consumption for health/well-being. Excludes botanical extracts used as drugs under D&C Act.

Q162
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: FSSAI Prohibited Claims

Question: Can an Ayurveda Aahar product claim to cure Type 2 Diabetes on its front label?

Benchmark Answer:

Strictly Prohibited. Regulation 5 of FSSAI (Ayurveda Aahar) Rules prohibits any disease prevention, treatment, or cure claims. Only structure/function or general health maintenance claims approved by FSSAI/Ayush expert committees are permitted.

Q163
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Mandatory Packaging Logos

Question: What visual identifier is legally mandatory on all packaged Ayurveda Aahar items?

Benchmark Answer:

The designated Ayurveda Aahar Logo (consisting of the symbolic green leaf and Ayurvedic pot motif) alongside the regular FSSAI green food logo and license number.

Q164
Jurisdiction Focus: India

Domain: Drugs & Magic Remedies Act

Question: What is the legal implication of advertising an Ayurvedic formulation as a "guaranteed cure for Cancer"?

Violation of Section 3 & 4 of the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954, read with its Schedule (which lists Cancer, Diabetes, Obesity, Cataract, etc.).
